## 🎙️ How call transcripts help

A transcript makes a recording easier to use after the call ends. You can read what was said instead of listening to the entire call again. You can scan for names, dates, promises, next steps, and other details. You can keep a written record for your own reference.

This is helpful when a call covers several topics. A normal conversation can move from one subject to another quickly. A transcript gives you a stable text version you can review at your own pace. A summary gives you a shorter starting point before you read the full transcript.

The app is designed for recorded phone calls. The recording is the input. The transcript is the readable result. The summary is the short overview.

## 🗣️ Speaker labels make conversations easier to read

In a two-person call, a wall of text can be hard to follow. Speaker diarization helps the transcript show who said what. Instead of one long block, the transcript can separate statements by speaker.

For a non-technical user, this means less confusion. If you are reviewing a call with a family member, a colleague, or a service provider, speaker labels make it easier to see the flow of the conversation. You can tell which person raised a topic, which person answered, and where a decision was made.

Speaker diarization is not about identifying people by name automatically. It is about separating voices in the transcript so the conversation reads more naturally.

## 📝 Summaries save time

A summary is a shorter version of the call. It helps you remember the main points without reading every word. longhand can create a summary from the call content on the device.

Summaries are useful for calls with several details. You might have a call about an appointment, a delivery, a family plan, or a work task. A summary gives you a quick view of what mattered. You can then open the full transcript when you need exact wording.

The summary is not a replacement for the transcript. It is a second view. The transcript gives you the full text. The summary gives you the quick overview.

## 💡 Tips for clearer transcripts

Use the best recording quality available. Speak clearly during the call, and reduce background noise when possible. A cleaner recording usually gives a clearer transcript.

Review the transcript alongside the summary. The summary helps you find the main points quickly, while the transcript gives you the exact words. Speaker labels are most useful when two people speak in turn.

Keep your recordings organized. A simple naming habit, such as the date and the person or topic, makes it easier to find a transcript later. Store the recordings and transcripts in a place you control.
